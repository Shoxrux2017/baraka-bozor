# BarakaBozor — Decision Log

## Purpose

One place for every decision that shapes the product or the engineering and that the code alone would not explain. Newest entries at the bottom. An entry is never edited after the fact; a later entry supersedes it and says so.

Who decides what, since 2026-09-24:

- **The Project Owner** decides product and business behaviour: what the service does for Customers, Shoppers, Couriers and staff, what is charged, and what is promised. The Owner is consulted only on a product question that neither the documents nor the interview of 2026-09-24 covers.
- **The implementing agent** (Claude Code) decides everything engineering: schema shapes, API details, error codes, concurrency, idempotency, libraries, structure, tests, deployment. It records each such decision here with its reason, so the Owner can overrule it later.

Earlier history: `docs/CONTRACT_ALIGNMENT_REPORT.md` (AUD-001 to AUD-029) and `docs/SPEC_DECISIONS_BACKLOG.md` (S-1 to S-43) are frozen records of the period when every decision went to the Owner. Every open item of that backlog is resolved below.

## DL-1 — Single implementing agent, full delegation (2026-09-24, Owner)

The three-party model (a planning agent, an implementing agent, the Owner merging) is retired. Claude Code is the only implementing agent: it plans, implements, verifies, obtains an independent review from a clean subagent, merges on a green CI run, runs the real stack, and reports once per wave. The Owner keeps one thing: a manual check of the product in the app once per wave, from a checklist the agent writes.

**Why:** the Owner was answering a question per task, and most of those questions were engineering. **Consequence:** `AGENTS.md`, `tasks/README.md` and the wave bookkeeping are rewritten for one implementer; concurrent-track machinery (`tasks/OWNERSHIP.md`, per-track worktrees, path ownership) is retired.

## DL-2 — Product decisions of the 2026-09-24 interview (Owner)

Recorded in full in `docs/INTERVIEW_2026-09-24.md`; `docs/01–09` are rewritten to match. The changes that alter the previously locked model:

1. **No prepaid flow.** Every order is paid after shopping. The Customer chooses cash to the Courier or online payment at checkout. Online: 30 minutes to pay after shopping completes, then Operator attention; the Operator may switch the order to cash or cancel it.
2. **Two price modes,** `fixed` and `estimate`, replacing `fixed`/`range`/`at_purchase`. `estimate` pays the actual purchase price; above the tolerance percentage the Shopper needs Customer approval first.
3. **Markup on goods** as a business setting, plus service fee and delivery fee.
4. **Cash accepted;** manual refunds tracked by the system; Payme and Click first, Paynet and xazna later.
5. **Web panel in the browser** for Operator, Admin and Manager instead of a Windows desktop application. Mobile stays Android and iOS.
6. **Admin does everything at launch;** Operator is a restricted Admin; Manager KPI screens deferred; Operators may assign; self-orders allowed with an audit flag.
7. **Order editing** by the Customer until shopping starts; minimum order amount; approval timing and cancellation rules as documented.
8. **Delivery:** handoff point by the market, several orders per Shopper, ASAP inside working hours plus a free-text wish, service area as a circle, Yandex MapKit, one fixed tariff, "not delivered" Courier action.
9. **Notifications:** push only; Courier gets push; Shopper polls in the foreground and gets push; Shopper calls the Customer from the app; Telegram Gateway for login codes first, Eskiz SMS fallback later; test phone numbers with a fixed code.
10. **Uzbek in Latin script,** in-app language switch defaulting to the device language, neutral design without brand assets.
11. **Cash-first pilot** as the order of work; hosting in Uzbekistan; personal Google Play account with closed testing now.

## DL-4 — Order lifecycle after the payment change (2026-09-24, agent)

States: `new, shopping_assigned, shopping, final_payment_pending, ready_for_delivery, delivery_assigned, on_the_way, completed, cancelled`. `checkout_payment_pending` is gone (no prepaid flow) and `approval_required` is derived (DL-3, S-6).

- Cash order: `shopping` → `ready_for_delivery` when shopping completes; the Courier records cash received when marking delivered.
- Online order: `shopping` → `final_payment_pending` when shopping completes; provider-confirmed payment → `ready_for_delivery`; 30 minutes unpaid → Operator attention; Operator may switch the order to cash (→ `ready_for_delivery`) or cancel.
- "Not delivered": `on_the_way` → `ready_for_delivery`, assignment ended with `delivery_failed`, Operator attention.
- Editing: allowed while `new` or `shopping_assigned` and shopping has not started; each edit re-snapshots the changed lines and writes history.

## DL-6 — What an order edit re-snapshots (2026-09-24, agent)

The Owner allowed editing until shopping starts (topic 4.2) without saying what gets re-priced. Decided: a line the Customer **adds** takes the current catalog price; a line that stays, with or without a quantity change, **keeps** its unit-price snapshot; a removed line is removed. The markup, tolerance, service fee, delivery fee and delay-threshold snapshots stay as at creation. The minimum order amount is re-checked against the new subtotal. **Why:** a price shown at order time is a promise; re-pricing untouched lines on an unrelated edit would surprise the Customer, and refreshing fee snapshots would let a settings change reach an existing order through the back door.

## DL-8 — Error-envelope details from W0-1 (2026-09-24, agent)

Found while building the renderer and its review: (1) planned maintenance answers `503 service_unavailable`, distinct from `provider_unavailable`, because "try again later" text for a provider outage would mislead during downtime; the client acts the same on both. (2) A business refusal (`ApiException`) is an expected outcome and is not reported to the error log; the response's `request_id` still lets support find the request's other log lines. (3) The test suite refuses to migrate any database whose name does not end in `_test`, and `phpunit.xml` sets the connection values in `$_SERVER` as well as `$_ENV`, because Laravel reads `$_SERVER` first and a forced `<env>` alone cannot beat an exported variable. (4) The request identifier is carried in the framework's request `Context`, so it also stamps the log lines of jobs the request queues.

## DL-9 — A password change revokes the account's other tokens (2026-09-24, agent)

`docs/09` Section 10 said nothing about other sessions. Decided: a successful password change deletes every other token of the account and keeps only the one that made the change. **Why:** the reason a person changes a password is usually that someone else may know it; leaving that someone's session alive would defeat the change. The cost is one re-login on a second device, which is what every mainstream service does. A blocked account still loses every token including the current one (`docs/07` Section 8).

## DL-10 — Login-code mechanics from W0-3 (2026-09-24, agent)

(1) A code is stored as a bcrypt hash and checked like a password. (2) A new request for a phone invalidates every earlier open challenge, so only the newest code works. (3) Verification checks in this order: no open challenge → `code_invalid`; expired → `code_expired`; five failures already → `code_attempts_exhausted`; hash mismatch → `code_invalid` and one more failure counted; only then the account, so `account_blocked` needs a valid code. (4) A configured test phone gets a stored challenge for the fixed code with `channel = test` and no delivery, so verification is one path for every phone and the send counters apply to test phones too. (5) A delivery failure stores no challenge and answers `503 provider_unavailable`; the provider's text goes to the log only.

## DL-11 — Login-code limits and guards from the W0-3 review (2026-09-24, agent)

(1) The send counters are incremented before delivery and the new count decides, so a parallel burst cannot pass a limit together, and a delivery that fails still costs its slot: a failing provider is retried at the same pace as a working one. (2) A fourth bound, ten code requests per address per minute, so one source cannot pump codes at many phones; the address comes through the trusted-proxy configuration like the staff-login limit. (3) The log records only the gateway class on a delivery failure; provider text is never logged, because an adapter's transport error can carry the request it was making and that request carries the code. (4) Test phones are resolved at boot; any test phone in a production environment refuses to boot, and production must name its code driver explicitly. (5) When only blocked Customer accounts hold a phone, verification answers `account_blocked` and creates nothing; `docs/09` Section 7's "creates one when none is active" is read together with its next sentence, never on its own. (6) Two simultaneous first logins for one phone are serialized by the row lock on the challenge; the second sees a consumed challenge and gets `code_invalid`, and the partial unique index on active Customers is the backstop.

## DL-12 — How the Operator surface is expressed (2026-09-24, agent)

The Owner made the Operator a restricted Admin (topic 6.2). Expressed as a rule over routes rather than a separate capability map: every route under `/api/v1/operations` admits both `operator` and `admin`, and no route anywhere else admits the Operator; admin-only routes live under `/api/v1/admin`. A structural test over the route table enforces the rule, so an admin-only route accidentally written with the Operator in its list fails the suite instead of passing review. Two further structural rules from the same review: a role check may only appear after the token, account-status and password-gate checks, and every route parameter is a constrained UUID (`UuidRouteParameters`) so a malformed id is a scope-safe 404 rather than a database error.

## DL-13 — Client session and language foundation from W0-5 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) Two Flutter packages added, `flutter_localizations` and `intl`, because Flutter's own `gen-l10n` needs them; the generated localization classes are committed under `lib/core/localization/generated/` so a build never depends on a generation step it might skip. (2) A request names the session it acts for: the active mode by default, an explicit slot for cross-mode calls, or none for the public endpoints; the interceptor never sends a staff token to a customer endpoint or the reverse. (3) A refused token drops its own session only; the other session, if any, continues in its mode without a server round trip. (4) At bootstrap an unreachable server keeps the stored tokens and shows a retry, because signing a Shopper out for a lost connection would lose an order in progress; a token the server refuses is cleared at once. (5) With both sessions stored the staff mode opens first. (6) The language preference lives in the same platform-secured storage as the tokens, under a prefixed key, to avoid a second storage plugin; it is reported to the server through `PATCH /auth/me` for every confirmed session, and a failure to report is not surfaced.

## DL-14 — Client session safety after the W0-5 review (2026-09-26, agent)

Supersedes point (2) of `DL-13` and adds to it. (1) The interceptor is endpoint-blind: the data source names the session a request acts for (`RequestSlot.of()`), and the interceptor sends what it is told. It is the data source, not the interceptor, that keeps a staff token off a customer endpoint. (2) The slot is decided once, when the request is sent, and stamped on the request; the error path reads only the stamp, so a mode switch while a request is in flight cannot make its refusal drop the other session. (3) A `401 authentication_required` or `401 account_blocked` drops the session only when the token that was sent is still the one stored for that slot; a refusal of a token that a new login has since replaced says nothing about the new session. (4) A result that arrives after its session was dropped or replaced is discarded: the controller applies a server answer to a session only while that session still holds the same user. (5) At bootstrap a stored token the server cannot confirm — anything but a `401` — keeps every token and reports the whole bootstrap as unreachable, so a session is never shown as absent while its token is stored; after a login from that state with a token in the other slot, both are confirmed together. (6) The client renders failure text from the machine `code` through one mapper (`core/localization/failure_text.dart`) that falls back on the HTTP status, then on a generic text, so nothing is ever shown raw. (7) The password confirmation is sent as typed, never copied from the new password, so the server's confirmation check stays real. (8) Customer mode from the staff interface sends the code to the staff account's own phone, taken from the confirmed staff user, never typed. (9) On the web panel the token lives in browser storage through the same store port — the trade-off accepted in `DL-3` for an internal panel behind a login.

## DL-15 — Client navigation, auth screens and the web target from W0-6 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) One session guard in `core/routing/session_redirect.dart` decides every navigation from the session state and the surface: until the bootstrap answers, the bootstrap screen, and the retry screen when the bootstrap threw; signed out, only `/auth`, `/auth/code` and `/auth/staff`; unreachable, only `/auth/unreachable`; a role on the wrong surface, only `/auth/wrong-surface`; the first-login gate, only `/auth/change-password`; otherwise the active role's area, plus `/auth/customer-mode` for a Shopper or Courier without a customer session. The router re-evaluates the guard whenever the session changes, so no screen navigates after a login, a mode switch or a dropped session — the guard moves the interface. (2) The auth screens are flat routes, not pages stacked over one another, so no back gesture lands on a screen the guard would bounce away from; each screen carries its own way back. (3) The surface is decided at build time (`kIsWeb`) and exposed as a provider, so tests show either surface. (4) The six areas are entered through one placeholder shell per role under `features/shells/`; a feature that fills an area later declares its pages inside it. (5) The phone field fixes `+998` and takes nine digits, because the API accepts exactly that; the one thing it normalizes is a pasted or autofilled full number, whose leading `998` is dropped so it does not pass as nine digits starting with the country code; the code field takes six digits; the password fields apply the 10–128 rule locally before the server does. (6) One form mutation runs at a time and a superseded completion is discarded (`core/state/mutation_state.dart`); the code login keeps its own generation for the same reason, starts every new request from a clean state, and runs a resend countdown driven by the server's `resend_available_in_seconds`. Whatever an operation throws, the form is usable afterwards: a known failure is shown as itself, anything else as the generic text and reported to Flutter's error handler; a retry of the session bootstrap that throws is reported the same way and the retry screen offered again. A location no route serves goes back to the entry point rather than to go_router's English error page. (7) Customer mode from the staff shell asks for the code before opening the entry screen, so the screen never mutates state in a widget lifecycle. (8) The web platform files (`web/`) are added and the Windows ones removed, per `DL-2` item 5 and `docs/07` section 2; the panel manifest carries the product name and the seed colour. (9) A frontend CI job runs format, analyze, tests, the localization regeneration check and the web build on the pinned Flutter version, on every pull request like the backend job; the Owner adds it to the required checks.

## DL-17 — Wave 1 engineering decisions (2026-09-26, agent)

Taken while planning `tasks/WAVE_1.md`; each task records anything finer. (1) The wave's schema task creates every table the wave needs (`docs/08` Document Status: every table is created by the wave that first needs it); the `business_settings` singleton and the four `payment_provider_settings` rows are inserted by their migrations, so the application never has to create them and a fresh database is configured by Admin, not by code. (2) Money arithmetic is integer only: a percentage arrives as a decimal string with at most two places and is applied as basis points, and every half-up rounding goes through one `MoneyCalculator`. (3) The customer price is computed from the current markup on every read and never stored on the product (`docs/08` section 7). (4) Customer catalog endpoints require a Customer session; a Shopper looking for a replacement (Wave 3) is admitted by widening the role list then, not by an anonymous catalog now. (5) Product images live on Laravel's `public` disk under `products/<uuid>.<ext>` with a fresh key per upload, so the public URL changes whenever the image does; the previous file is removed after the new row is committed; S3-compatible storage replaces the disk at deployment through the same filesystem abstraction. (6) Deleting an address deactivates it, always: an address costs nothing to keep, and a hard delete for the unreferenced case would only tie Wave 1 to the `orders` table of Wave 2. (7) Address create and update answer `409 checkout_configuration_incomplete` while the service area is unset, the same code checkout will use, so the client has one text for "the business is not configured yet". (8) A temporary staff password is twelve characters from an alphabet without look-alikes (`23456789ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijkmnpqrstuvwxyz`), generated with `random_int`, returned once in the create or reset response and never logged; blocking and a password reset both delete every token of the account at once (`docs/02` section 12), because a reset is what an Admin does when the holder of the old sessions can no longer be trusted. (9) A push device is upserted on `(push_token, user_id)`, revoked rather than deleted, and revived by a new registration; the client registers through a `PushTokenSource` port whose real implementation is the FCM one of Wave 4, so Wave 1 ships the wiring and a fake that yields no token. (10) Search is `lower(column) LIKE lower(?)` with the pattern's wildcards escaped, over both name columns; a substring pattern is a sequential scan, which is fine for the assumed catalog size (interview 2.4), and the `lower()` indexes of `docs/08` serve equality and prefix lookups; lists are ordered by `sort_order` then `name_uz` so pagination is deterministic. (11) Backend modules for the wave: `Catalog`, `Settings`, `Customer`, `Staff`, `Notifications`, each with its route file and service provider under the registries of `docs/07` section 3. (12) The panel's screens are Material 3 forms and tables with no design system beyond the seed colour (interview 8.3), and every list the panel shows is server-paginated. (13) A route parameter is a constrained UUID (`DL-12`) or, from this wave, a closed enum constrained to its values by a route pattern, listed by name in `UuidRouteParameters` beside the UUID names so the structural route test still fails on an unconstrained parameter; `{provider}` of the payment-provider settings is the first. (14) Archive is a distinct state from hidden: archive sets `archived_at` and clears `is_active`, restore clears `archived_at` and sets `is_active` back to true, and Admin may clear `is_active` alone to hide a catalog entry without archiving it.

## DL-18 — The Wave 1 schema from W1-1 (2026-09-26, agent)

The seven tables follow `docs/08` sections 5 to 8, 11, 12 and 25, with these additions, each a structural invariant the database can hold for any writer (seeder, console, queue job) and now named in `docs/08`: (1) names are non-blank on categories and products, `varchar(120)` for categories and `varchar(160)` for products; (2) an archived category or product is never active, so archiving and hiding cannot disagree, and the catalog actions assign both flags explicitly rather than through `fill()`; (3) `business_settings` is a singleton (`id = 1`), every amount and percentage is non-negative, the radius is `numeric(6,2) > 0`, the delay threshold is positive, the centre lies within coordinate ranges, and working hours come as a pair — so a request that breaks one of these must be refused by validation first, never reach PostgreSQL; (4) a push token is non-blank and at most 512 characters; (5) the image size limit is 1 byte to 5 MiB inclusive. The `payment_provider_settings` key stays a plain string in the model: an enum cast on a primary key breaks Eloquent's key handling (`find`, `whereKey`, collection lookups), so the enum is offered by a method. Which units take decimal quantities arrives with the cart (Wave 2), not with the unit list.

## DL-19 — Money core and business settings from W1-2 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) A percentage is a `Percentage` value of integer basis points, parsed from the two-decimal string the API and the `numeric(5,2)` columns carry; `MoneyCalculator::increaseByPercent` forms `amount × (10000 + bp)` exactly and rounds once, half up, to 1 UZS. Only what Wave 1 uses exists; the service-fee percentage, line totals and the tolerance ceiling join it in the waves that compute them. (2) `CustomerPriceCalculator` is built once per request from the stored markup and applied to every product in the response. (3) `ServiceAreaPolicy` measures the haversine distance with the IUGG mean Earth radius, 6371.0088 km, and refuses with `422 address_outside_service_area` carrying `max_distance_km` and `distance_km` as two-decimal strings, or with `409 checkout_configuration_incomplete` while the centre or the radius is unset. (4) `PATCH /admin/settings/business` takes any subset; money is a strict JSON integer, percentages, coordinates and the radius are decimal strings, never JSON numbers; the subset is merged into the locked singleton row and the cross-field rules — the value of the service-fee mode not chosen must be null, working hours and the centre come in pairs, opening and closing differ — are checked on the merged row and reported as `validation_failed` on the field that has to change. Closing after midnight is allowed; what it means for collection is Wave 2's `WorkingHours`. (5) The payment-provider list is the fixed four in the order the interview names them, answered in the collection envelope of `docs/09` section 2 as one page, so the panel parses every list one way; `is_enabled` is a strict boolean. (6) Validation bounds: money at most 1 000 000 000 UZS, coordinates at most six decimals, the radius at most 9999.99 km, the delay threshold 1 to 1440 minutes; an empty PATCH body is refused, which also refuses a body the JSON decoder could not read. (7) A refused distance is shown rounded up, so it never reads equal to the radius. (8) Opening and closing must differ, so a business open round the clock enters 00:00–23:59; Wave 2's `WorkingHours` treats that pair as always open.

## DL-24 — Malformed input is refused before the application sees it (2026-09-26, agent)

One global middleware answers `400 malformed_request` for (1) a JSON body the decoder cannot read — Laravel would otherwise pass it on as an empty body, so a truncated request would be judged as one that sent nothing — and (2) text that is not UTF-8 or holds a NUL byte, in the query string, the body or an uploaded file's name, keys included. PostgreSQL refuses invalid UTF-8 with an error that would surface as `500`, and the database driver cuts a string at a NUL and would store half a value. No honest client sends either, so the answer names a transport fault rather than a field. The middleware sits after CORS, maintenance and the body-size check, so the refusal is readable by the web panel from its own origin and a too-large body is still `413`, and before the trimming, so it judges the input as sent; the framework has no "insert before", so `bootstrap/app.php` lists the global stack whole and a test pins it to the framework's default. The refusal is a plain HTTP `400`: the API renderer gives it the envelope, and outside the API the framework renders it like any `400`, never a `500`. The URL path is checked earlier by the framework's own `ValidatePathEncoding`; its `400` now gets the envelope too, because the renderer decides whether a request is for the API on the raw path — `is('api/*')` matches the decoded path with a UTF-8 pattern and fails on exactly such a path. An empty body stays empty and goes to the endpoint's own validation. This supersedes the last clause of `DL-19` (6): an unreadable body is no longer refused as an empty PATCH (`422` on `body`) but as `400 malformed_request`, on every endpoint.

## DL-37 — Wave 2 engineering decisions (2026-09-27, agent)

Taken while planning `tasks/WAVE_2.md` and revised by its review; each task records anything finer.

1. **Modules.** One backend module, `Orders`, holds the cart, checkout, the Customer's orders and the operations board, and Wave 3's shopping and delivery actions join it: they share the order aggregate and its locks (`docs/07` section 16), and splitting them would move the lock order across module boundaries. Wave 5's `Payments` is its own module for the provider adapters and calls the `Orders` actions for the order's transitions under the order lock. The idempotency store is shared infrastructure in `App\Support\Idempotency`.
2. **Order number.** `orders_order_number_seq` starts at 1001, so the first number already has the four digits of the interview's example (topic 9.0); numbers are sequential and unique, not gapless — a rolled-back creation leaves a gap, which `BR-ORDER-005` allows.
3. **Online payment.** In this wave `online` is refused at preview and creation with `409 payment_method_unavailable` whatever the provider switches say, because no adapter exists before Wave 5 (`docs/06` section 2: online shown as unavailable); Wave 5 makes "available" mean "enabled and an adapter registered".
4. **Checkout token.** Stateless. It carries in the clear a version, the Customer id, the cart id, the address id, the payment method, the delivery wish and the expiry (five minutes, `BR-CHK-006`), plus a digest of what it binds: each cart line's product, `unit_code`, quantity, note and rule, each product's price mode and current customer price, the address point, street and house, and the settings the order will snapshot. It is signed with HMAC-SHA256 under a purpose key derived from the application key (`HMAC(APP_KEY, "checkout-token/v1")`, so the MAC never shares a key with Laravel's encrypter) and compared with `hash_equals`. Order creation recomputes the digest from the values it snapshots; any difference, an expiry, another Customer or a bad signature is `409 checkout_snapshot_stale`. Rotating the application key makes tokens in flight stale, which costs a new preview. Replay is covered by the idempotency key and the cart's conversion.
5. **Idempotency.** `begin` commits the `processing` row in its own short transaction, so the lease, the takeover and `409 idempotency_in_progress` of `docs/07` section 17 are real states; the operation's writes and `complete` then commit together, so no completed key exists without its effect; a business refusal deletes the row, so a retry with the same key is judged again. Each `begin` and each takeover writes a fresh `attempt_token` on the row, and both `complete` and the refusal's delete act only on a row still `processing` with that token, rolling back otherwise — so an attempt that outran its lease can neither complete over nor delete the record of the attempt that took over. The request hash is SHA-256 over the canonical JSON — keys sorted — of the operation name, the route parameters and the validated body, so one key sent to cancel two different orders is `409 idempotency_key_reused`, not a replay of the first. The key must be a UUID (`docs/08` section 27).
6. **Bounds.** A quantity is at most 9 999.999 for `kg`, `liter` and `meter` and 9 999 for the whole-number units (`422 validation_failed` on the quantity); a cart holds at most 100 lines (`409 cart_full` for the 101st) and an order edit's list at most 100 (`422` on `items`). These are technical ceilings no household order reaches, keeping every line total far inside the integer range; they are not a typo guard.
7. **Cart lock.** Every cart mutation locks the active cart row first (`FOR UPDATE`) and re-checks that it is active; order creation takes the same lock, reads the lines, their products and the settings once, and builds both the digest and the snapshots from those same values, so a cart write lands either before the lock, where it makes the token stale, or after it: an add looks the active cart up again and lands in the new one, while a change or removal of a line whose cart was converted is the scope-safe `404`. Lock order: the cart, then the order (`docs/07` section 16).
8. **Markup per line.** Each order line records the markup it was priced with, `order_items.markup_percent_snapshot`: at creation the order's; for a line added by an edit, the current one, because the interview (topic 4.2) prices added lines at current catalog prices and an estimate line's bill must use the markup its estimate was made with (`BR-PRICE-003`). The order-level markup snapshot stays as at creation (`DL-6`) as the record of the markup the order was placed under, but no bill is computed from it: every estimate bill, from Wave 3 on, uses the line's; a replacement is billed with its original line's markup.
9. **Edit history.** Lines an edit removes become `removed` with `customer_removed` and `removed_at`, billable quantity and line total zero, never deleted (`docs/08` section 1). The `edited` history row carries a structured `details` (`order_history.details jsonb`): the lines added, the lines removed, and each kept line's quantity, note and rule before and after, and the delivery wish before and after (`docs/05` section 22). Kept lines are matched only among the lines not removed, so a product removed in one edit and added again in a later one is a new line at the current price and markup, not the old snapshot revived; the client's editor starts from the lines not removed. An edit that changes nothing is a natural repeat with no history row.
10. **Totals before completion.** Until shopping completes, an order's totals are computed from its snapshots over the lines not removed — the merchandise as the sum of `half_up(customer_unit_price_uzs_snapshot × ordered_quantity)`, the service fee by the snapshotted rule, the delivery fee snapshot — and labelled like the preview (`BR-CHK-005`): `estimate` when any line is an estimate, `final` when every line is fixed, which says the prices are guaranteed, not that nothing can be removed. From completion they are the stored final amounts and the kind is `final`. A cancelled order carries no amount due, whatever its lines (`docs/04` section 10, `BR-CAN-006`): its totals are `null` with the kind `none`, and the client says it is cancelled with nothing to pay. The board shows the same.
11. **Pickers.** The Operator picks a Shopper from `GET /operations/shoppers`: active Shoppers with their count of current assignments, paginated and ordered by name, for Operator and Admin, because `/admin/staff` is Admin-only (`DL-12`); Wave 3 adds `GET /operations/couriers` the same way.
12. **Courier assignment in Wave 3.** It is built where an order first reaches `ready_for_delivery`, so it is written and verified with the flow that reaches it; this supersedes the "assignment" of `DL-5`'s Wave 2 in that part, and `docs/06` says so.
13. **Direct cancellation.** It takes an optional `reason` of up to 300 characters; the cancellation request of Wave 3 requires one, because an Operator decides on it. Pending lines become `removed` with `order_cancelled`. Cancelling an already cancelled order is a natural repeat. The request branch for `shopping` through `delivery_assigned` arrives with Wave 3.
14. **Shopper assignment.** `POST` makes the first assignment and `PUT` a reassignment; assigning the current Shopper again is a natural repeat; an id that is not a Shopper account is `422 validation_failed` on `shopper_id`, a blocked Shopper `409 staff_not_active`. The history row's `details` carry the assignment id and `is_self_order` (interview topic 6.4).
15. **No push in this wave.** The notification events and their sender are Wave 4's (`docs/06`); the actions of this wave gain their notification calls then.
16. **Board, summary, attention.** The board filters by `status`, `shopper_id`, `payment_method`, `from` and `to` (dates of `created_at` in `Asia/Tashkent`) and `attention`, and its `search` matches the order number or the Customer's phone digits or name; `courier_id` arrives with Wave 3. The summary counts every open order by its current status whatever day it was placed — an order placed overnight is collected in the morning (`BR-CHK-009`) — and today's completed and cancelled orders by `completed_at` and `cancelled_at`, the day in `Asia/Tashkent`; today's sales are the sum of `final_total_uzs` of orders completed today (`docs/05` section 20); and the number of attention items. The attention list holds what the wave can produce: a `self_order` item while an order is open and its current Shopper assignment is a self-order; each other type arrives with the wave that creates its state.
17. **Panel areas.** The board is feature `operations` under `/operations`; an Admin's area is `/admin` and `/operations`, an Operator's only `/operations`; the Admin lands on the board after sign-in and its shell lists the board first beside its own sections, the Operator's lists only the board (`docs/02` section 7). This supersedes `DL-27` (1)'s landing page, which was to give way to the board. The session guard keeps the location a start asked for through the bootstrap and returns to it when the role may see it, which closes the Wave 1 risk of a reload landing on the area's home.
18. **Customer mode.** It exists for a Shopper or Courier since Wave 0 (`DL-15` (7)); this wave gives it a cart to reach, and the closure walks it.
19. **Money on the client.** The client shows the money the server computed — the cart's estimate subtotal, the preview's totals, the order's totals — and never sums prices itself.
20. **Unavailable products in the cart.** A line whose product became unavailable stays with `is_available: false` and outside `estimated_subtotal_uzs`, so the Customer sees why checkout refuses it; adding a hidden product and adding an id that does not exist both answer `409 product_unavailable`, keeping `DL-22` (2).
21. **Checkout check order.** The settings' completeness is checked first, because the service-area and minimum checks need it; then the checks in the order `docs/09` section 18 lists its codes. `00:00`–`23:59` is always open (`DL-19` (8)).

## DL-38 — The Wave 2 schema from W2-1 (2026-09-27, agent)

The seven tables follow `docs/08` sections 9, 10, 13 to 16 and 27 with `DL-37`'s additions (`order_items.markup_percent_snapshot`, `order_history.details`, `idempotency_keys.attempt_token`) and these, each an invariant the database can hold for any writer. (1) What a status implies is a check on `orders`, not only an action's habit: a shopping order has its start; an order past shopping has its completion instant and its final amounts; readiness, departure and completion have their instants; `completed_at` belongs to `completed` alone and `cancelled_at` with a reason to `cancelled` alone. ## DL-39 — The idempotency store from W2-2 (2026-09-27, agent)

`App\Support\Idempotency\IdempotencyStore::run()` takes the actor, a stable operation name (`orders.create`, `orders.cancel`), the key, the request hash of `RequestFingerprint::of(operation, route parameters, validated body)`, the operation as a closure that returns the resource it produced, and a closure that loads a resource by id. (1) A replay returns the resource as it is now, loaded fresh, and the controller answers it with the status of the original success (`201` for a creation): `docs/07` section 17 asks for the same logical result, and a stored response would show an order as it was, not as it is. A replay does not run the operation, so it skips every check inside it; the replay closure therefore loads through the actor's current scope (`ScopedLookup`), and a record the actor may no longer see — a Shopper since reassigned — is the scope-safe `404`, while the key stays completed. The key records the resource's table and id; an operation whose answer is not one resource — Wave 4's reorder — needs more, which the Wave 2 risk list carries. (2) The operation runs in one transaction with `complete()`; the key was committed as `processing` before it, in its own transaction, by `begin()`. Any failure — a refusal or an error — deletes the key through `abandon()`, so a retry is judged again; if the deletion itself fails, it is reported, the original failure is what the caller sees, and the key waits out its lease. `run()` is called at transaction level 0: inside an outer transaction `begin()` would be a savepoint, with no committed lease. Only effects that roll back with the transaction run inside the operation; a push, a queued job or a provider call is dispatched after commit from inside it, so neither a rollback nor a replay repeats it. (3) `complete()` and `abandon()` act only on a row still `processing` with the attempt's own token; an attempt that lost its key to a takeover gets `409 idempotency_in_progress`, and its transaction rolls back. (4) When the insert conflicts and the existing row has meanwhile been deleted by a refused attempt, the answer is `409 idempotency_in_progress`, and the client's retry begins afresh; looping inside the request would buy little. (5) The header is checked by route middleware, `RequireIdempotencyKey`: missing or blank is `400 idempotency_key_required`, not a UUID `422 validation_failed` on `Idempotency-Key`, and the action reads the key, lower-cased, through `RequireIdempotencyKey::of()`. (6) Two requests with one key at the same instant are held apart by PostgreSQL — the insert's `on conflict do nothing` against the unique key, then `FOR UPDATE` on the existing row. One test class runs without a wrapping transaction, with real commits and a second connection: the key `begin()` commits is visible to another request before the operation starts; a refused operation leaves neither key nor effect; a second request waits on the first's uncommitted key and then finds it in progress; a late completion waits on an uncommitted takeover and then loses without erasing it. The other tests put the row in each remaining state. `begin()`, `complete()` and `abandon()` are public beside `run()` so these steps can be driven one by one. (7) The request fingerprint compares route parameters in lower case, so one id in either case is one request, and refuses an object anywhere in the request, since a bound model would hash its current attributes.

## DL-40 — The cart from W2-3 (2026-09-27, agent)

(1) Every cart call answers the whole cart, so the client redraws it from one response: `201` for an added line, `200` for a change and for a removal, which is why a removal answers the cart rather than `204`. (2) A quantity is held as whole thousandths (`App\Support\Money\Quantity`) and a line total is `MoneyCalculator::lineTotal`, half-up to 1 UZS, so the cart's estimate is computed the way an order's line will be. Which units take a fraction is `UnitCode::allowsFraction()` (`kg`, `liter`, `meter`); the rules and bounds of `DL-37` (6) are `Orders\QuantityPolicy`. A whole unit's quantity is sent and answered without a fraction (`"2"`, and `"2.0"` is refused), a fractional unit's with up to three decimals and answered with three (`"1.500"`), and a quantity sent as a JSON number is refused (`docs/07` section 14). (3) The duplicate refusal names the existing line (`details.cart_item_id`), so the client opens it instead of adding; `cart_full` names the limit; `product_unavailable` names the product. (4) A line whose product became unavailable stays, with no price and outside the subtotal; it may be removed, not changed, since changing it would need the product the Customer may no longer see. (5) The cart lock of `DL-37` (7) is `CustomerCart::lock()`: an insert that does nothing when an active cart exists, then `FOR UPDATE`, inside a transaction. A locked read that waited while an order converted the cart comes back empty — PostgreSQL re-checks the row, now `converted`, and drops it, and the new cart was inserted after the statement began — so `lock()` reads once more with a new statement, which sees the new cart; the change then lands there, and a change or removal of an old line finds it gone and is the scope-safe `404`. The duplicate check under the lock and `cart_items (cart_id, product_id)` together keep one line per product, and two adds of one product at once make one line and tell the second which. (6) The cart's computation — availability, the current price, each estimate and the subtotal — is `Orders\CartView`, which the resource only formats and checkout reads again.

## DL-41 — The checkout preview from W2-4 (2026-09-27, agent)

(1) `Orders\Checkout\CheckoutState::gather()` reads the settings once and decides every check from that one reading, in the order of `DL-37` (21); order creation (W2-5) calls it again under the cart lock and snapshots from it. `ServiceAreaPolicy::assertDeliverableUnder()` takes the settings already read. (2) The statuses: `409` for every refusal about the state of the business or the Customer's own data — configuration, name, address incomplete, empty cart, unavailable product, minimum, payment method — and `422 address_outside_service_area`, as the address API already answers it; an address that is not the Customer's own active one is the scope-safe `404`. (3) Settings are complete for checkout (`BR-SET-002`) when the service fee of the chosen mode, the delivery fee, the minimum, both working hours and the whole service area are set; the markup, the tolerance and the delay threshold always have values. (4) A line whose stored quantity no longer fits its product's unit — an Admin changed `kg` to `piece` under a `1.500` line — is refused with the unavailable ones (`QuantityPolicy::fits`), so the order never snapshots 1.5 pieces. (5) The minimum is checked on the merchandise subtotal, before fees (`BR-CHK-003`); the service fee is on the same subtotal (`ServiceFeeCalculator`, `MoneyCalculator::percentOf`, half-up). (6) `WorkingHours` is in `Asia/Tashkent`, open from the opening minute up to, not including, the closing one; a closing time before the opening spans midnight, and `00:00`–`23:59` is always open, when `opens_at` is `null` in the preview. (7) The token is `base64url(claims).base64url(HMAC-SHA256)`, the claims in the clear as `DL-37` (4) lists them, signed under `HMAC(APP_KEY, "checkout-token/v1")` and compared as text, so a token has one spelling. The digest is itself keyed — `HMAC(APP_KEY, "checkout-digest/v1")` over the canonical JSON — which refines `DL-37` (4): the Customer reads the digest in the token and knows every bound value but the market prices, the markup, the tolerance and the delay threshold, so a plain SHA-256 of the public layout let the market price and the markup be recovered by trying candidates offline (found by the review in 0.01 s). Both keys come from `CheckoutSecrets`, which fails closed on an empty or undecodable `APP_KEY`. The digest covers everything the order would snapshot: the recipient's name and phone, each line's product names, unit, price mode, market and customer price, quantity, note and rule, the whole address, the payment method, the delivery wish, and the snapshotted settings. Reading a token checks the signature (`hash_equals`), the version, the owner and the expiry, and any failure is `409 checkout_snapshot_stale`. The canonical JSON under both the digest and the idempotency fingerprint is one helper, `App\Support\CanonicalJson`. (8) A subtotal can grow past what a naive percentage can hold within the cart's bounds, so `MoneyCalculator::percentOf` divides before it multiplies and is exact for any amount, and every sum of amounts goes through `MoneyCalculator::sum`, which refuses to overflow rather than let PHP turn it into a float.

## DL-42 — Order creation and the Customer's orders from W2-5 (2026-09-27, agent)

(1) The token is read inside the idempotent operation, not before it, so a retry after the token has expired still replays the order the first request created; a token refused inside the operation deletes the key like any refusal, and a retry is judged again. (2) Under the cart lock, a cart other than the token's means the token's cart has been ordered since: the token is stale. That is also what a second creation that waited on the first finds — the first converted the cart, and the second's locked read reaches the new one (`DL-40` (5)) — proven by a test that runs the second creation in another process while a second connection converts the cart. (3) The checkout is checked again from the locked cart with `CheckoutState::gather()`; a check that now fails answers its own code (a product gone is `409 product_unavailable`, naming it), because that tells the Customer what changed; everything else that changed is a digest mismatch and `409 checkout_snapshot_stale`. (4) Every snapshot is taken from the values the digest was computed over: the recipient as the Customer's name and phone, the address, the settings, and each line's names, unit, price mode, market and customer price and the current markup (`DL-37` (8)); the first history row is `status_changed` to `new` by the Customer; the order number is read back after the insert. (5) The Customer's list answers a summary per order — enough to find it and see where it stands — and the detail the whole resource, so a long history does not carry every line of every order; the list's line count and totals are sums in SQL (`CustomerOrders::withLineSums`, `round()` half-up as `DL-38` (2)), so a page does not load its orders' lines. An address removed between the preview and the creation makes the token stale rather than answering a bare `404` to a request that names no address. (6) The resource shows what exists: `pending_approval_count` is `0`, `payment` `null`, `refunds` empty and `can_request_cancellation` `false` until the waves that bring approvals, payments, refunds and the cancellation request; `can_edit` and `can_cancel_directly` follow `OrderPermissions::canChange`, the rule of `BR-ORDER-004` and `BR-CAN-001`. (7) An item shows its snapshotted price and, until it is bought, that price times the ordered quantity as its total; removed lines stay in the list with their status, and the client leaves out the ones the Customer removed.

## DL-43 — Editing and cancelling an order from W2-6 (2026-09-27, agent)

(1) An edit is a `PUT` of the whole item list and the delivery wish, without an `Idempotency-Key` (`docs/09` section 48 does not list it): sending the same list twice is a natural repeat, so a retry needs no key; a retry that arrives after shopping started answers `409 order_editing_locked`, and the client reloads the order. The request carries no version, so of two edits from two devices at once the later wins; only the Customer edits, before shopping, so this is accepted. Product ids are compared in lower case. The delivery wish must be sent — `null` clears it — so a client that forgets it is refused rather than wiping it; a line's note and rule default to none and `allow_similar_substitution`, since the list states the order as the Customer wants it. (2) Lines are matched by product among the lines not removed. A line that stays keeps its price and markup (`DL-6`) and its quantity is held to the unit it was ordered in, even if the product's unit or availability has since changed: the snapshot is the promise, and a product gone since is the Shopper's to find unavailable. An added line must be a product the Customer may see now, at the current price and markup (`DL-37` (8)). (3) The edit is planned in full before anything is written. A plan that changes nothing returns the order — a natural repeat, whatever the settings say now. A plan that lowers the merchandise subtotal must still reach the current `minimum_order_uzs` (the order does not snapshot it; a business with none set checks none); a plan that does not lower it is never refused for it. So a minimum raised after the order was placed never freezes the order, the same principle as `DL-6`: a settings change does not reach an existing order through the back door. A refused edit writes nothing. Edits are throttled to 30 a minute per Customer, since each may keep a removed line. (4) The `edited` history row's `details` hold `added` (with each new line's price and markup), `removed` and `changed` (each kept line's quantity, note and rule before and after) and, when it changed, `delivery_time_note` before and after (`DL-37` (9)). (5) A direct cancellation is idempotent through the store (`DL-39`), keyed on the order; it removes the open lines with `order_cancelled`, ends the current Shopper assignment with `order_cancelled`, keeps the Customer's reason as the history row's note, and answers the order; a cancelled order is a natural repeat, whatever cancelled it. From `shopping` on it answers `409 order_cancellation_not_allowed` until Wave 3 brings the request. (6) Both answer `200` with the Customer's order resource, from the one rule `OrderPermissions::canChange`.
