# BarakaBozor — Decision Log

## Purpose

One place for every decision that shapes the product or the engineering and that the code alone would not explain. Newest entries at the bottom. An entry is never edited after the fact; a later entry supersedes it and says so.

Who decides what, since 2026-09-24:

- **The Project Owner** decides product and business behaviour: what the service does for Customers, Shoppers, Couriers and staff, what is charged, and what is promised. The Owner is consulted only on a product question that neither the documents nor the interview of 2026-09-24 covers.
- **The implementing agent** (Claude Code) decides everything engineering: schema shapes, API details, error codes, concurrency, idempotency, libraries, structure, tests, deployment. It records each such decision here with its reason, so the Owner can overrule it later.

Earlier history: `docs/CONTRACT_ALIGNMENT_REPORT.md` (AUD-001 to AUD-029) and `docs/SPEC_DECISIONS_BACKLOG.md` (S-1 to S-43) are frozen records of the period when every decision went to the Owner. Every open item of that backlog is resolved below.

## DL-1 — Single implementing agent, full delegation (2026-09-24, Owner)

The three-party model (a planning agent, an implementing agent, the Owner merging) is retired. Claude Code is the only implementing agent: it plans, implements, verifies, obtains an independent review from a clean subagent, merges on a green CI run, runs the real stack, and reports once per wave. The Owner keeps one thing: a manual check of the product in the app once per wave, from a checklist the agent writes.

**Why:** the Owner was answering a question per task, and most of those questions were engineering. **Consequence:** `AGENTS.md`, `tasks/README.md` and the wave bookkeeping are rewritten for one implementer; concurrent-track machinery (`tasks/OWNERSHIP.md`, per-track worktrees, path ownership) is retired.

## DL-2 — Product decisions of the 2026-09-24 interview (Owner)

Recorded in full in `docs/INTERVIEW_2026-09-24.md`; `docs/01–09` are rewritten to match. The changes that alter the previously locked model:

1. **No prepaid flow.** Every order is paid after shopping. The Customer chooses cash to the Courier or online payment at checkout. Online: 30 minutes to pay after shopping completes, then Operator attention; the Operator may switch the order to cash or cancel it.
2. **Two price modes,** `fixed` and `estimate`, replacing `fixed`/`range`/`at_purchase`. `estimate` pays the actual purchase price; above the tolerance percentage the Shopper needs Customer approval first.
3. **Markup on goods** as a business setting, plus service fee and delivery fee.
4. **Cash accepted;** manual refunds tracked by the system; Payme and Click first, Paynet and xazna later.
5. **Web panel in the browser** for Operator, Admin and Manager instead of a Windows desktop application. Mobile stays Android and iOS.
6. **Admin does everything at launch;** Operator is a restricted Admin; Manager KPI screens deferred; Operators may assign; self-orders allowed with an audit flag.
7. **Order editing** by the Customer until shopping starts; minimum order amount; approval timing and cancellation rules as documented.
8. **Delivery:** handoff point by the market, several orders per Shopper, ASAP inside working hours plus a free-text wish, service area as a circle, Yandex MapKit, one fixed tariff, "not delivered" Courier action.
9. **Notifications:** push only; Courier gets push; Shopper polls in the foreground and gets push; Shopper calls the Customer from the app; Telegram Gateway for login codes first, Eskiz SMS fallback later; test phone numbers with a fixed code.
10. **Uzbek in Latin script,** in-app language switch defaulting to the device language, neutral design without brand assets.
11. **Cash-first pilot** as the order of work; hosting in Uzbekistan; personal Google Play account with closed testing now.

## DL-4 — Order lifecycle after the payment change (2026-09-24, agent)

States: `new, shopping_assigned, shopping, final_payment_pending, ready_for_delivery, delivery_assigned, on_the_way, completed, cancelled`. `checkout_payment_pending` is gone (no prepaid flow) and `approval_required` is derived (DL-3, S-6).

- Cash order: `shopping` → `ready_for_delivery` when shopping completes; the Courier records cash received when marking delivered.
- Online order: `shopping` → `final_payment_pending` when shopping completes; provider-confirmed payment → `ready_for_delivery`; 30 minutes unpaid → Operator attention; Operator may switch the order to cash (→ `ready_for_delivery`) or cancel.
- "Not delivered": `on_the_way` → `ready_for_delivery`, assignment ended with `delivery_failed`, Operator attention.
- Editing: allowed while `new` or `shopping_assigned` and shopping has not started; each edit re-snapshots the changed lines and writes history.

## DL-6 — What an order edit re-snapshots (2026-09-24, agent)

The Owner allowed editing until shopping starts (topic 4.2) without saying what gets re-priced. Decided: a line the Customer **adds** takes the current catalog price; a line that stays, with or without a quantity change, **keeps** its unit-price snapshot; a removed line is removed. The markup, tolerance, service fee, delivery fee and delay-threshold snapshots stay as at creation. The minimum order amount is re-checked against the new subtotal. **Why:** a price shown at order time is a promise; re-pricing untouched lines on an unrelated edit would surprise the Customer, and refreshing fee snapshots would let a settings change reach an existing order through the back door.

## DL-8 — Error-envelope details from W0-1 (2026-09-24, agent)

Found while building the renderer and its review: (1) planned maintenance answers `503 service_unavailable`, distinct from `provider_unavailable`, because "try again later" text for a provider outage would mislead during downtime; the client acts the same on both. (2) A business refusal (`ApiException`) is an expected outcome and is not reported to the error log; the response's `request_id` still lets support find the request's other log lines. (3) The test suite refuses to migrate any database whose name does not end in `_test`, and `phpunit.xml` sets the connection values in `$_SERVER` as well as `$_ENV`, because Laravel reads `$_SERVER` first and a forced `<env>` alone cannot beat an exported variable. (4) The request identifier is carried in the framework's request `Context`, so it also stamps the log lines of jobs the request queues.

## DL-9 — A password change revokes the account's other tokens (2026-09-24, agent)

`docs/09` Section 10 said nothing about other sessions. Decided: a successful password change deletes every other token of the account and keeps only the one that made the change. **Why:** the reason a person changes a password is usually that someone else may know it; leaving that someone's session alive would defeat the change. The cost is one re-login on a second device, which is what every mainstream service does. A blocked account still loses every token including the current one (`docs/07` Section 8).

## DL-10 — Login-code mechanics from W0-3 (2026-09-24, agent)

(1) A code is stored as a bcrypt hash and checked like a password. (2) A new request for a phone invalidates every earlier open challenge, so only the newest code works. (3) Verification checks in this order: no open challenge → `code_invalid`; expired → `code_expired`; five failures already → `code_attempts_exhausted`; hash mismatch → `code_invalid` and one more failure counted; only then the account, so `account_blocked` needs a valid code. (4) A configured test phone gets a stored challenge for the fixed code with `channel = test` and no delivery, so verification is one path for every phone and the send counters apply to test phones too. (5) A delivery failure stores no challenge and answers `503 provider_unavailable`; the provider's text goes to the log only.

## DL-11 — Login-code limits and guards from the W0-3 review (2026-09-24, agent)

(1) The send counters are incremented before delivery and the new count decides, so a parallel burst cannot pass a limit together, and a delivery that fails still costs its slot: a failing provider is retried at the same pace as a working one. (2) A fourth bound, ten code requests per address per minute, so one source cannot pump codes at many phones; the address comes through the trusted-proxy configuration like the staff-login limit. (3) The log records only the gateway class on a delivery failure; provider text is never logged, because an adapter's transport error can carry the request it was making and that request carries the code. (4) Test phones are resolved at boot; any test phone in a production environment refuses to boot, and production must name its code driver explicitly. (5) When only blocked Customer accounts hold a phone, verification answers `account_blocked` and creates nothing; `docs/09` Section 7's "creates one when none is active" is read together with its next sentence, never on its own. (6) Two simultaneous first logins for one phone are serialized by the row lock on the challenge; the second sees a consumed challenge and gets `code_invalid`, and the partial unique index on active Customers is the backstop.

## DL-12 — How the Operator surface is expressed (2026-09-24, agent)

The Owner made the Operator a restricted Admin (topic 6.2). Expressed as a rule over routes rather than a separate capability map: every route under `/api/v1/operations` admits both `operator` and `admin`, and no route anywhere else admits the Operator; admin-only routes live under `/api/v1/admin`. A structural test over the route table enforces the rule, so an admin-only route accidentally written with the Operator in its list fails the suite instead of passing review. Two further structural rules from the same review: a role check may only appear after the token, account-status and password-gate checks, and every route parameter is a constrained UUID (`UuidRouteParameters`) so a malformed id is a scope-safe 404 rather than a database error.

## DL-13 — Client session and language foundation from W0-5 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) Two Flutter packages added, `flutter_localizations` and `intl`, because Flutter's own `gen-l10n` needs them; the generated localization classes are committed under `lib/core/localization/generated/` so a build never depends on a generation step it might skip. (2) A request names the session it acts for: the active mode by default, an explicit slot for cross-mode calls, or none for the public endpoints; the interceptor never sends a staff token to a customer endpoint or the reverse. (3) A refused token drops its own session only; the other session, if any, continues in its mode without a server round trip. (4) At bootstrap an unreachable server keeps the stored tokens and shows a retry, because signing a Shopper out for a lost connection would lose an order in progress; a token the server refuses is cleared at once. (5) With both sessions stored the staff mode opens first. (6) The language preference lives in the same platform-secured storage as the tokens, under a prefixed key, to avoid a second storage plugin; it is reported to the server through `PATCH /auth/me` for every confirmed session, and a failure to report is not surfaced.

## DL-14 — Client session safety after the W0-5 review (2026-09-26, agent)

Supersedes point (2) of `DL-13` and adds to it. (1) The interceptor is endpoint-blind: the data source names the session a request acts for (`RequestSlot.of()`), and the interceptor sends what it is told. It is the data source, not the interceptor, that keeps a staff token off a customer endpoint. (2) The slot is decided once, when the request is sent, and stamped on the request; the error path reads only the stamp, so a mode switch while a request is in flight cannot make its refusal drop the other session. (3) A `401 authentication_required` or `401 account_blocked` drops the session only when the token that was sent is still the one stored for that slot; a refusal of a token that a new login has since replaced says nothing about the new session. (4) A result that arrives after its session was dropped or replaced is discarded: the controller applies a server answer to a session only while that session still holds the same user. (5) At bootstrap a stored token the server cannot confirm — anything but a `401` — keeps every token and reports the whole bootstrap as unreachable, so a session is never shown as absent while its token is stored; after a login from that state with a token in the other slot, both are confirmed together. (6) The client renders failure text from the machine `code` through one mapper (`core/localization/failure_text.dart`) that falls back on the HTTP status, then on a generic text, so nothing is ever shown raw. (7) The password confirmation is sent as typed, never copied from the new password, so the server's confirmation check stays real. (8) Customer mode from the staff interface sends the code to the staff account's own phone, taken from the confirmed staff user, never typed. (9) On the web panel the token lives in browser storage through the same store port — the trade-off accepted in `DL-3` for an internal panel behind a login.

## DL-15 — Client navigation, auth screens and the web target from W0-6 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) One session guard in `core/routing/session_redirect.dart` decides every navigation from the session state and the surface: until the bootstrap answers, the bootstrap screen, and the retry screen when the bootstrap threw; signed out, only `/auth`, `/auth/code` and `/auth/staff`; unreachable, only `/auth/unreachable`; a role on the wrong surface, only `/auth/wrong-surface`; the first-login gate, only `/auth/change-password`; otherwise the active role's area, plus `/auth/customer-mode` for a Shopper or Courier without a customer session. The router re-evaluates the guard whenever the session changes, so no screen navigates after a login, a mode switch or a dropped session — the guard moves the interface. (2) The auth screens are flat routes, not pages stacked over one another, so no back gesture lands on a screen the guard would bounce away from; each screen carries its own way back. (3) The surface is decided at build time (`kIsWeb`) and exposed as a provider, so tests show either surface. (4) The six areas are entered through one placeholder shell per role under `features/shells/`; a feature that fills an area later declares its pages inside it. (5) The phone field fixes `+998` and takes nine digits, because the API accepts exactly that; the one thing it normalizes is a pasted or autofilled full number, whose leading `998` is dropped so it does not pass as nine digits starting with the country code; the code field takes six digits; the password fields apply the 10–128 rule locally before the server does. (6) One form mutation runs at a time and a superseded completion is discarded (`core/state/mutation_state.dart`); the code login keeps its own generation for the same reason, starts every new request from a clean state, and runs a resend countdown driven by the server's `resend_available_in_seconds`. Whatever an operation throws, the form is usable afterwards: a known failure is shown as itself, anything else as the generic text and reported to Flutter's error handler; a retry of the session bootstrap that throws is reported the same way and the retry screen offered again. A location no route serves goes back to the entry point rather than to go_router's English error page. (7) Customer mode from the staff shell asks for the code before opening the entry screen, so the screen never mutates state in a widget lifecycle. (8) The web platform files (`web/`) are added and the Windows ones removed, per `DL-2` item 5 and `docs/07` section 2; the panel manifest carries the product name and the seed colour. (9) A frontend CI job runs format, analyze, tests, the localization regeneration check and the web build on the pinned Flutter version, on every pull request like the backend job; the Owner adds it to the required checks.

## DL-17 — Wave 1 engineering decisions (2026-09-26, agent)

Taken while planning `tasks/WAVE_1.md`; each task records anything finer. (1) The wave's schema task creates every table the wave needs (`docs/08` Document Status: every table is created by the wave that first needs it); the `business_settings` singleton and the four `payment_provider_settings` rows are inserted by their migrations, so the application never has to create them and a fresh database is configured by Admin, not by code. (2) Money arithmetic is integer only: a percentage arrives as a decimal string with at most two places and is applied as basis points, and every half-up rounding goes through one `MoneyCalculator`. (3) The customer price is computed from the current markup on every read and never stored on the product (`docs/08` section 7). (4) Customer catalog endpoints require a Customer session; a Shopper looking for a replacement (Wave 3) is admitted by widening the role list then, not by an anonymous catalog now. (5) Product images live on Laravel's `public` disk under `products/<uuid>.<ext>` with a fresh key per upload, so the public URL changes whenever the image does; the previous file is removed after the new row is committed; S3-compatible storage replaces the disk at deployment through the same filesystem abstraction. (6) Deleting an address deactivates it, always: an address costs nothing to keep, and a hard delete for the unreferenced case would only tie Wave 1 to the `orders` table of Wave 2. (7) Address create and update answer `409 checkout_configuration_incomplete` while the service area is unset, the same code checkout will use, so the client has one text for "the business is not configured yet". (8) A temporary staff password is twelve characters from an alphabet without look-alikes (`23456789ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijkmnpqrstuvwxyz`), generated with `random_int`, returned once in the create or reset response and never logged; blocking and a password reset both delete every token of the account at once (`docs/02` section 12), because a reset is what an Admin does when the holder of the old sessions can no longer be trusted. (9) A push device is upserted on `(push_token, user_id)`, revoked rather than deleted, and revived by a new registration; the client registers through a `PushTokenSource` port whose real implementation is the FCM one of Wave 4, so Wave 1 ships the wiring and a fake that yields no token. (10) Search is `lower(column) LIKE lower(?)` with the pattern's wildcards escaped, over both name columns; a substring pattern is a sequential scan, which is fine for the assumed catalog size (interview 2.4), and the `lower()` indexes of `docs/08` serve equality and prefix lookups; lists are ordered by `sort_order` then `name_uz` so pagination is deterministic. (11) Backend modules for the wave: `Catalog`, `Settings`, `Customer`, `Staff`, `Notifications`, each with its route file and service provider under the registries of `docs/07` section 3. (12) The panel's screens are Material 3 forms and tables with no design system beyond the seed colour (interview 8.3), and every list the panel shows is server-paginated. (13) A route parameter is a constrained UUID (`DL-12`) or, from this wave, a closed enum constrained to its values by a route pattern, listed by name in `UuidRouteParameters` beside the UUID names so the structural route test still fails on an unconstrained parameter; `{provider}` of the payment-provider settings is the first. (14) Archive is a distinct state from hidden: archive sets `archived_at` and clears `is_active`, restore clears `archived_at` and sets `is_active` back to true, and Admin may clear `is_active` alone to hide a catalog entry without archiving it.

## DL-18 — The Wave 1 schema from W1-1 (2026-09-26, agent)

The seven tables follow `docs/08` sections 5 to 8, 11, 12 and 25, with these additions, each a structural invariant the database can hold for any writer (seeder, console, queue job) and now named in `docs/08`: (1) names are non-blank on categories and products, `varchar(120)` for categories and `varchar(160)` for products; (2) an archived category or product is never active, so archiving and hiding cannot disagree, and the catalog actions assign both flags explicitly rather than through `fill()`; (3) `business_settings` is a singleton (`id = 1`), every amount and percentage is non-negative, the radius is `numeric(6,2) > 0`, the delay threshold is positive, the centre lies within coordinate ranges, and working hours come as a pair — so a request that breaks one of these must be refused by validation first, never reach PostgreSQL; (4) a push token is non-blank and at most 512 characters; (5) the image size limit is 1 byte to 5 MiB inclusive. The `payment_provider_settings` key stays a plain string in the model: an enum cast on a primary key breaks Eloquent's key handling (`find`, `whereKey`, collection lookups), so the enum is offered by a method. Which units take decimal quantities arrives with the cart (Wave 2), not with the unit list.

## DL-19 — Money core and business settings from W1-2 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) A percentage is a `Percentage` value of integer basis points, parsed from the two-decimal string the API and the `numeric(5,2)` columns carry; `MoneyCalculator::increaseByPercent` forms `amount × (10000 + bp)` exactly and rounds once, half up, to 1 UZS. Only what Wave 1 uses exists; the service-fee percentage, line totals and the tolerance ceiling join it in the waves that compute them. (2) `CustomerPriceCalculator` is built once per request from the stored markup and applied to every product in the response. (3) `ServiceAreaPolicy` measures the haversine distance with the IUGG mean Earth radius, 6371.0088 km, and refuses with `422 address_outside_service_area` carrying `max_distance_km` and `distance_km` as two-decimal strings, or with `409 checkout_configuration_incomplete` while the centre or the radius is unset. (4) `PATCH /admin/settings/business` takes any subset; money is a strict JSON integer, percentages, coordinates and the radius are decimal strings, never JSON numbers; the subset is merged into the locked singleton row and the cross-field rules — the value of the service-fee mode not chosen must be null, working hours and the centre come in pairs, opening and closing differ — are checked on the merged row and reported as `validation_failed` on the field that has to change. Closing after midnight is allowed; what it means for collection is Wave 2's `WorkingHours`. (5) The payment-provider list is the fixed four in the order the interview names them, answered in the collection envelope of `docs/09` section 2 as one page, so the panel parses every list one way; `is_enabled` is a strict boolean. (6) Validation bounds: money at most 1 000 000 000 UZS, coordinates at most six decimals, the radius at most 9999.99 km, the delay threshold 1 to 1440 minutes; an empty PATCH body is refused, which also refuses a body the JSON decoder could not read. (7) A refused distance is shown rounded up, so it never reads equal to the radius. (8) Opening and closing must differ, so a business open round the clock enters 00:00–23:59; Wave 2's `WorkingHours` treats that pair as always open.

## DL-20 — The Admin catalog API from W1-3 (2026-09-26, agent)

(1) Every paginated list answers the envelope of `docs/09` section 2 through the `PaginatedResponse` introduced in W1-2, not Laravel's paginated resource shape. (2) A list query is validated by a lenient form request (`ListRequest`): `page` 1 to 100 000, `per_page` 1 to 100, and the list's filters; an unknown query parameter is ignored, and a parameter sent empty means absent, because the strict request shape of `docs/09` section 4 governs mutation bodies; a flag takes `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`; leading zeros are not integers. (3) Search is a substring match over both names that ignores case, reads `ё` as `е`, and reads the Uzbek apostrophe's four other forms (ʻ ‘ ’ `) as `'`; PostgreSQL folds both sides (`translate(lower(…))`), so the database must run a UTF-8 character type (`docs/08` section 1, guarded by a test); the term's `%`, `_` and `\` are escaped; a blank term is no filter; at most 100 characters. Columns are qualified so a caller may join. (4) Whether a product's category exists and is open is decided by the action under a share lock, not by the form request, so a category archived at the same moment is decided in one order; an edit that re-sends the product's current category is not a move and is allowed even when that category has since been archived. (5) Every catalog write runs in a transaction on the locked row — an edit, an archive, a restore — so a stale read never reaches the archived-implies-inactive check as a `500`; lock order is the entry, then the category. (6) Bounds: names 1–120 (categories) and 1–160 (products) characters, descriptions at most 2000, `sort_order` −100 000 to 100 000, market price 1 to 1 000 000 000 UZS. (7) An empty PATCH body is refused on every PATCH endpoint (`RefusesEmptyPatch`, generalising `DL-19` (6)). (8) Admin responses show `market_price_uzs` and the `customer_unit_price_uzs` it becomes under the current markup; the calculator is built once per response. The image fields arrive with W1-4, additively.
