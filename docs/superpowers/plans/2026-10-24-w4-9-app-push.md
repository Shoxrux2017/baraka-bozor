# W4-9 Push in the App Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** The Android app receives the pushes W4-7 and W4-8 send: it registers its Firebase token for every signed-in account and again when the token changes, shows a banner in the foreground unless the order is already on screen, asks for the notification permission once after the first sign-in into a role that receives pushes, and a tap opens the order in the event's mode, from a cold start too.

**Architecture:** The Flutter code depends on three ports in `features/notifications/domain/push.dart`: `PushTokenSource` (exists, gains a change stream), `PushInbox` (arrived, opened and launch pushes, the permission) and `BannerDisplay` (the channel, the foreground banner, its taps). Firebase and `flutter_local_notifications` live only in two `data/` adapters and in `push_setup.dart`, which `main()` calls before `runApp` and which returns provider overrides; without Firebase options, and on the web, the defaults stay `NoPush…` and nothing touches a plugin. Three application objects, kept alive by the app root beside the existing `PushRegistrar`: `PushPresenter` (foreground banners, the channel's name, routing taps), `PushOpener` (holds a tapped target until the session can open it, switches mode, navigates) and `PushPermissionPrompt` (asks once). The server gains one thing: the delivery id becomes the notification's tag, so a resend replaces the banner instead of adding one.

**Tech Stack:** Flutter 3.47 / Dart 3.13, Riverpod 3, GoRouter 18; new `firebase_core` ^5.1.0, `firebase_messaging` ^17.0.1, `flutter_local_notifications` ^22.3.2. Laravel 13 for the tag.

**Spec:** `docs/DECISIONS.md` `DL-104` (12), `DL-112`, `DL-114` (3) and (4); `tasks/WAVE_4.md` W4-9 and the W4-8 P3 row; `docs/07-architecture.md` sections 2, 8 and 24; `docs/05-business-rules.md` section 18 (the thirteen events and their recipients). This plan's decisions become `DL-115` (Task 7). One may correct `DL-104` (12): if the options alone do not deliver to a killed app, the native setup comes from the same dart-defines through Gradle `resValue`, not from a `google-services.json` and the Google Services plugin (Task 2 decides).

## Review Focus

1. **A cold-start tap lands on the home screen.** The bootstrap's redirect and the opener's navigation race; the order must open over its list once the session is known (Task 4, `'a tap that launched the app opens the order once the session is known'`).
2. **The wrong mode or someone else's order.** A Shopper's push tapped while the phone is in Customer mode must switch to the staff mode; a Courier event on a phone whose staff slot is a Shopper, or a Customer event with no Customer session, opens nothing (Task 4).
3. **A rotated token leaves an account unregistered, or a logout revokes the stale device.** A token change while two accounts are signed in re-registers both; the next logout revokes the newer device (Task 1).
4. **Two banners for one push.** A resend after a lost answer (W4-8 P3) and a foreground banner followed by the system's own must replace each other: the tag is the delivery id on the server and on the foreground banner (Tasks 3 and 6).
5. **The permission asked at launch, twice, or never.** Not before a session passes its gates (first-login password, staff step, the Customer's name), never for a panel role, once per install even across restarts, and a build without push neither asks nor marks it asked (Task 5).
